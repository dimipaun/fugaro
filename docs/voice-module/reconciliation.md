## 1. How this spec and the evaluation switch unify — one decision, two documents

The spec's **"engine toggle and experiments"** and **"keys and security"** sections and the design on **PR #633** — merged, on `main` as `80f2b7fa` — are **the same subject seen from two sides**: the design's, which asks whether this may leave the machine at all, under what conditions, and how the answer is carried, and the module's, which asks what the switch does and what the rest of the module does while it is on. This document treats them as one subject with one authority rule:

> **[voice-evaluation-switch.md](../mobile/design/voice-evaluation-switch.md) (PR #633) is authoritative for *what is permitted*: its seven points are conditions on any remote voice work in this repository, console or phone, and this spec may not be built in a way that breaks one of them. [spec.md](spec.md) is authoritative for *what is built*: where it answers one of the design's questions (Q0–Q4), its answer supersedes that question; where it does not, the design's own answer of 2026-10-19 governs and the question is not re-asked here.**

The design's questions are now **Q0–Q4**, and its own §11 records what changed to get there: it renamed its file and **consolidated its first revision's six questions into four** — the first revision's **Q1**, D44's second explicit egress decision, **is no longer written as a question** but as a decision still owed; the two questions its hop dissolved (how the phone learns the policy, and the fifth read family) are gone with the consumer-delivered decision; its key-holder question became **Q2**; its persistence question stands as **Q3** — so **Q4 is the one question carried forward from that revision**, and **Q0** is the document's own: the acknowledgement of its §1a amendment. **All five are answered** — the owner answered Q0–Q4 on 2026-10-19, each taking the option the design recommended — so the table below asks a different question of each row than it did in the first revision: **what does this spec say about a matter the design has now settled**, where it once asked what the design left open. ## 3. Findings

Each finding is classified **Wrong** (the spec, or a document here, says something the code contradicts), **Exists** (the tree has it and the spec should be read as naming it), **Gap** (nothing in the tree does it), **Rule** (not a fact but a constraint the tree's existing decisions impose on how this may be built), or **Open** (a question this document cannot answer from the tree alone).

### (a) The mobile voice work, the shared module, and the switch

**#1 — The spec is a second edition of a document this repository already committed and already reconciled. (Exists)**

[voice-spec.md](../design/voice-spec.md) is the owner's voice spec of 2026-10-15, committed verbatim; [voice.md](../design/voice.md) reads it against the console and the mobile work and carries **F1–F38** and **D39–D54b**, every one answered by the owner on 2026-10-16. The shared text is substantial: both have the goal ("hands-free"), both name the pipeline and reject speech-to-speech, both list multi-user voice and voice-driven code editing as non-goals, and `voice-spec.md` §4's component table (`AudioIn`, `SpeechToText`, `TextToSpeech`, `AgentBridge`, `Summarizer`, `EventDigester`, `VoiceController`) is the architecture the new text describes in prose. **What the new text changes** is the vendor list, the runtime engine toggle, the wake word (a non-goal in 2026-10-15, in scope in 2026-10-18), and the divorce of the interfaces from the `AgentBridge` naming.

**Consequence.** The two texts must not both be authoritative. This document treats the 2026-10-18 spec as the **later** one for what the module *does*, and voice.md's D39–D54b as standing until the owner supersedes a row of it — the way mobile D129 requires supersessions to be recorded. **Nothing in voice.md is reversed here**; §5 Q9 asked the owner for the rule, and the owner answered it on 2026-10-19 — option (1), with the amendment left to a separate docs-only change.

**#2 — "One common interface per direction" already exists, twice, and the two disagree about how many ports there are. (Exists)**

`voice.md` **D52** names **four**: `AudioIn`, `SpeechToText`, `TextToSpeech` and `AudioOut` — the fourth being what plays speech out, which matters because §g's echo-cancellation rule needs a *writable* audio path, not just a readable one. **Merged with this change** (`mobile/src/audio/`) implements **three** — `isAudioIn`, `isSpeechToText`, `isTextToSpeech` in `mobile/src/audio/{audio-in,stt,tts}.js` — and folds output into `TextToSpeech` itself. `mobile/implementation-plan.md`'s P3.4 verification says "The interfaces exist (AudioIn, STT, TTS)"; `AudioOut` is not in it.

Two further facts about those ports decide how the spec's "audio frames in → partial/final transcripts out" maps onto them. First, **they are shape predicates, not TypeScript interfaces**: *"There is no TypeScript here and no `interface` keyword: a port is an object with methods … and `isAudioIn` is that check."* Second, **audio does not cross any port**: `AUDIO_IN_EVENT_FIELDS` is `['kind']` — the events carry no samples — and `stt.js` states the rule, *"Text crosses this port. Audio never does."* That is D118's answer, and it is why the spec's "audio frames in" has no existing carrier on the phone side.

**Consequence.** The module's one-interface-per-direction claim is satisfiable, but it is satisfied by **the ports that exist**, and the spec's frame-level phrasing needs a decision about which process holds frames (see #21 and #39). The `AudioOut` gap is real: whichever document is authoritative for the port list, one of them is wrong about the fourth.

**#3 — The spec puts the module in the console; the tree moved it out, deliberately. (Wrong)**

The spec's first sentence — "starting as a separable module in the Electron console, later in the phone app" — is the placement `voice.md` **D50** originally chose, and it was **amended** by mobile **D119**: *"A pure-logic package in this repository, in its own top-level directory — and that is an amendment of D50"*, because there are now two consumers and `console/` is outside the Go module with its own boundary tests. On `main` the result is visible: `shared/` holds `command`, `confirm`, `digest`, `lease`, `mailbox`, `mailbox-schema`, `mailbox-sim`, `presence`, `skeleton` and `url`.

**Consequence.** Building the module *inside* `console/` would place it on the wrong side of a boundary the repository tests for (`internal/cli/docs_console_boundary_test.go`: `TestConsoleIsOutsideTheGoModule`, `TestGoModHasNoConsoleDependency`). The module belongs in `shared/`; the console and the phone are its two consumers.

**#4 — "App stays in the foreground while talking (no background listening)" collides with the mobile work's background audio mode. (Open)**

The spec's *Goal* states it, in one sentence ("App stays in the foreground while talking (no background listening)"). The mobile plan's **Check 86** is "Hands-free audio: **the background mode**, the microphone ask, Bluetooth, and barge-in", reserved for P3.5/P4.2, and `mobile/reconciliation.md`'s **U7** asks *"Whether the OS grants background audio with barge-in on both platforms"* — i.e. the tree assumes a background audio mode the spec's sentence excludes. On iOS "background mode" and "stays in the foreground" are not the same axis, so the two can be reconciled — but not by a reader guessing. §5 Q4 asked it, and the owner answered on 2026-10-19 — option (1), foreground only.

**#5 — The Swift helper the spec asks for has no carrier, and the console is built to have exactly one child. (Gap)**

`console/src/main/spawn.ts` opens: *"The console spawns exactly one binary — the installed `fugaro` — and never reads a database, a config file or a credential itself."* `console/package.json`'s `build.files` lists `dist/main/**`, two preload files, `dist/shared/**`, `dist/source/**` and five `dist/bundle/` files — **no second executable** — and there is no `extraResources`, `extraFiles`, `asarUnpack` or `binaries` key (asserted absent by the console's own tests). The design's answer to whether the app needs a second process already exists though, and it is not the console's own: `voice.md` **D40** puts the Agent SDK in a **separate helper process outside main and renderer**, supervised by the console, using the operator's own Claude login. The spec's Swift helper is the same shape for a different job.

**Consequence.** A Swift audio helper is a **nested, separately signed, separately notarised binary inside the bundle** — a new packaging and release surface, not a new file. See #17 for what that costs and §5 Q6 for whether it should be a second helper or the same one — the owner's answer of 2026-10-19 is one helper, extended (option (1)).

**#6 — The spec's cloud engines presuppose the one decision the tree has deliberately not taken. (Rule)**

`voice.md` **D44** is answered, verbatim: *"**Local-only for v1** … Cloud STT/TTS is out of v1; it happens only by a **second, explicit owner decision**"*, with *"absent means nothing leaves the machine"* and a default-deny allow-list whose defaults are *"the opposite of `allow_data_to`'s"*. The design on `main` (`80f2b7fa`) is where that second decision is written down, and it is not built. Its current revision states the decision as **still owed and still untaken** rather than as one of its questions — *"the owner's ruling of 2026-10-19 permits the app to carry the switch **so that the decision can be taken by use**; it is not that decision"* — and puts its own gate on a different question, **Q0**, the acknowledgement of its §1a amendment to `mobile/implementation-plan.md`'s Global Constraint 12. **The owner gave that acknowledgement on 2026-10-19 — option (a), yes as described — and the plan's constraint 12 now carries the dated note recording it, in place, with the original sentence still readable.**

**Consequence.** The spec's *Speech-to-text*, *Text-to-speech* and *Engine toggle* sections are **conditioned on a decision that has not been taken**. This document does not take it, and does not read the spec as taking it. §5 Q1, answered by the owner on 2026-10-19 (§5a): the evaluation switch, not the egress decision.

**#7 — The wake word already exists as a transcript prefix test, not an acoustic engine. (Exists / Rule)**

The spec asks for an acoustic detector (openWakeWord or Porcupine) that "routes speech to the app vs the active agent". The tree already has the routing, and it is by text: `shared/command/index.js` exposes `WAKE_ALIASES` — `Object.freeze(['fugaro', 'figaro'])` — and `routeUtterance`, whose rule is that an utterance whose first token is in the closed alias set is an app command and any other utterance is agent text. `mobile/functional-spec-v0.1.md` §3.4 states it ("Any utterance starting with 'Fugaro' is handled by the app"), `functional-spec-reconciliation.md` **F5/D164** reconciles it, and P6.1's verification pins it: *"an unmatched 'Fugaro …' yields 'not understood' and is never forwarded"*, plus *"a test pins that 'yes' is not an alias of 'confirm'"*.

Note the cost the tree already recorded: the reconciliation found that the spec's **"never misroutes"** claim **is not true**, which is why the recognition rate is proposed live Check 102.

**Consequence.** Two different mechanisms wearing one word. An acoustic engine firing on the wake word is a **second, independent** trigger whose failures are not the transcript test's failures — and it would necessarily run before any transcript exists, which is exactly the audio path #21 says has no carrier. §5 Q5, answered by the owner on 2026-10-19: option (1), no acoustic engine.

**#8 — A runtime "cloud icon" is not the same object as the allow-list, and the tree has rules about both. (Rule / Open)**

The spec's *Engine toggle* section — "Cloud icon on the voice screen switches local/cloud (three-way on Mac: Apple, whisper.cpp, cloud)" — is a **per-session choice by a person**. The design on `main` (`80f2b7fa`) separates three things that the spec's one icon merges: the **allow-list** (§4.3: admitted by name — *"An entry is a provider name, never a URL"* — default-deny, *"Absent, empty, malformed or unreadable all mean **local only**"*), the **persistent indicator** (§4.2: it *"shows exactly one of **local**, **remote (<provider name>)**, **blocked by project policy**"*, and *"It is not dismissible and it is not a toast"*), and the **fail-closed ladder** (§4.5: *"Every row is **fail-closed to local**"*, including *"the network is down | local only, immediately; **nothing is queued for later**, because a queued utterance is a recording, and D118's answer forbids one"*).

**Consequence.** A toggle that a person can flip to cloud is exactly why the indicator needs a state that says the request was refused — the design's **blocked by project policy** — and the toggle may only ever **narrow** what the allow-list permits, never widen it. The spec's three-way distinction on Mac also implies the toggle must name *which* local engine, which the allow-list's shape (a list of provider names) does not express. §5 Q3, answered by the owner on 2026-10-19: option (1), the allow-list decides and the toggle may only narrow.

**#9 — The classifier the spec asks for exists, with a different and sharper taxonomy. (Exists)**

`shared/digest/severity.js` (merged on `main`) exports `SEVERITY` — `kill`, `failed`, `fast_burn`, `info` — `SEVERITY_ORDER` and `severityRank`, with the rule that *"a kill is the highest severity; a failed finish is next; a fast burn is next; nothing else interrupts."* `shared/digest/index.js`'s `digest(input)` maps the feed's shapes onto them: `JSONKill` → kill, `JSONFinished.Failed`/`.Status` → failed, `JSONBurn.FastAlert` → fast_burn, everything else → info. `shared/prompts` on `main` (`f67753f9`) carries the second half: `evaluateInterrupt(input)` with the `INTERRUPT` set (`kill`, `cap-reached`, `cap-near`, `offline`, `prompt`).

**Consequence.** The spec's three tiers (urgent / mention / silent) are **finer on the low end** (the tree's ladder has no "mention" rung — everything below `info` is silence, which is the spec's "silent" tier by default) and **coarser on the high end** (the tree has two urgent reasons the spec does not name: `fast_burn` and `offline`). §4 recommends keeping the tree's ladder and adding one rung, rather than replacing it.

**#10 — The debounce window and the on-demand digest are designed but not built. (Gap / Exists)**

`voice-spec.md` **§7** designs the digester, its rolling window and the on-demand digest; `voice.md` **D39** is the data-plane answer; `shared/digest` implements the *classification* half. What does **not** exist anywhere is the **debounce window** — nothing holds a burst open — and the **"what's going on?" query**, which has no command, no wire shape and no reply. `shared/prompts` on `main` (`f67753f9`) has the closest thing (a queue with `announcement` and `waitingSentence`), and it is a queue of *prompts*, not a window over *events*.

**Consequence.** The spec's §1–§2 are buildable on `shared/digest`; §3–§4 need a new component and a new wire shape, and §4 needs a route from a spoken question to an answer, which is a mailbox message (see #23) rather than anything that exists.

**#11 — The summariser is designed, unbuilt, and its model call is outside every Fugaro cap. (Exists / Rule)**

`voice-spec.md` §6: *"Model: a fast, cheap model (Haiku-class), configurable"*, with a config shape `summarizer: { model: <haiku-class>, max_sentences: 3 }` at **§10 Configuration** (line 137). `voice.md` **D45** is the answer that matters: the summariser's model call is **spend on the operator's own login** — D40's mechanism — and *"`fugaro budget show` will not show it and no Fugaro cap bounds it."* No `Summarizer` symbol exists in `internal/`, `shared/`, `console/src` or `mobile/src`. The word "haiku" does appear in the tree — as a **model alias** the runner and the config layer carry: `internal/config/providers.go`, `internal/config/pins.go`, `internal/pricing/alias.go`, `internal/pricing/canonical.go`, `internal/pricing/table.go`, `internal/runner/gateway.go`, `internal/runstore/runstore.go` and `internal/config/example.yaml` all name it, and `internal/agent/env.go` sets `ANTHROPIC_DEFAULT_HAIKU_MODEL` from the per-repo `background` model. Every one of those is a **run-time model choice**, not the voice summariser: no symbol summarises a turn.

**Consequence.** "Whose credential, capped how" has an answer already: **the operator's, and not capped** — disclosed by D45 and by mobile **D116** (the summariser's spend is disclosed but sits outside Fugaro's caps). The spec's *Keys and security* section must not be read as proposing a Fugaro-managed summariser key; see #28 for why the credentials service cannot become one, and §5 Q7 — where the owner answered on 2026-10-19 that there is no app-side cap (option (1)), only a running count.

**#12 — The interrupt switch exists, with three positions rather than two. (Exists)**

`shared/prompts` on `main` (`f67753f9`) (P6.5) exports `SWITCH` (`NARROW`, `DEFAULT`, `WIDEN`) and `INTERRUPT` (`KILL`, `CAP_REACHED`, `CAP_NEAR`, `OFFLINE`, `PROMPT`), with `evaluateInterrupt` returning `{ ok, interrupt, reason, pending }`. Its README states the default — *"**Queue, do not interrupt** is the spec's own default (§3.2)"* — the escalation ladder (re-announced after **one hour**, then once a day), and that the switch is a **local** setting: *"never written to the database."* Mobile **D127** is the decision row behind it.

**Consequence.** The spec's "urgent items cut in immediately or wait for a pause" is the two-position version of an already-designed three-position switch, and the design's extra position (`widen` → a pending `prompt` may interrupt) is the one the spec's own "agent asking a question" trigger needs. Build on `shared/prompts`; do not write a second switch.

### (b) The Electron console

**#13 — The console has no audio code at all, of any kind. (Gap)**

Grepping `console/src` for `getUserMedia`, `mediaDevices`, `AudioContext`, `MediaRecorder`, `microphone`, `audio`, `speech` and `voice` returns **nothing**. The only two hits for permission machinery are `console/src/main/window.ts`'s `denyEveryPermission(session: Session)`, whose two handlers are `setPermissionRequestHandler((_c, _p, cb) => cb(false))` and `setPermissionCheckHandler(() => false)`. The screens that exist are `live`, `projects`, `run`, `spend` and `degraded`.

**Consequence.** Every console-side claim in the spec — the Swift helper, the voice screen, the cloud icon — is greenfield, and it is greenfield in a window designed to deny everything.

**#14 — `connect-src 'none'` is the load-bearing directive, and the spec's "clients stream directly to providers" requires loosening it. (Rule)**

`console/src/main/protocol.ts` exports `CONTENT_SECURITY_POLICY`, byte-identical to the `<meta http-equiv>` in `console/src/renderer/index.html`:

```text
default-src 'none'; script-src 'self'; style-src 'self'; img-src 'self' data:; font-src 'self'; connect-src 'none'; form-action 'none'; base-uri 'none'; frame-ancestors 'none'; object-src 'none'
```

The comment above it is explicit: *"`connect-src 'none'` is the load-bearing one: the renderer …"*. There is **no `media-src`** directive (so it falls back to `default-src 'none'`), no `'unsafe-eval'` and no `'wasm-unsafe-eval'` — the console's own tests assert both absences (`window.test.ts` refuses `unsafe-inline`/`unsafe-eval`; `csp-document.test.ts` also refuses `unsafe-hashes`, `strict-dynamic`, `*` and `http:`). `console/README.md` lists `connect-src` relaxed to `'self'` as a **mutation** its frozen tests catch.

**Consequence.** A renderer that opens a WebSocket to `api.deepgram.com` or `api.cartesia.ai` needs `connect-src` to name those origins — a **loosening in the one window this repository hardens hardest**, and one the console's tests are written to refuse. The alternatives, with their real costs, are §5 Q2's options. The same sentence covers Kokoro: ONNX/WASM in the renderer needs `'wasm-unsafe-eval'` added to `script-src`, which is a second loosening for a different reason (#39).

**#15 — Capture is outside the renderer by ruling, and the renderer holds no permission. (Rule)**

`voice.md` **D43** is answered: *"the renderer gains no network path and no permission, and capture happens **outside** it"*, and §5.1 rule 9 of the console design *"is not moved by this work"* — the owner **refused** the one-window amendment. `denyEveryPermission` is the code that makes it true. **D51** adds: the minimum platform entitlement only in the phase that ships audio, with `NSMicrophoneUsageDescription` mandatory and `com.apple.security.device.audio-input` *"only if the chosen capture mechanism needs it"*, and the prompt tied to a **visible session start**, not to launch.

**Consequence.** `getUserMedia` in the console renderer is not merely unimplemented; it is **ruled out**. The spec's Chromium `echoCancellation` design (#38) therefore cannot live where the spec implies — see #39.

**#16 — The macOS bundle grants exactly one entitlement, and not the audio one. (Exists)**

`console/entitlements.mac.plist` grants one key: `com.apple.security.cs.allow-jit`. Its own prose withholds `disable-library-validation`, `allow-unsigned-executable-memory`, `allow-dyld-environment-variables`, `debugger`/`get-task-allow`, `app-sandbox` **and every device and network entitlement including `com.apple.security.device.audio-input`**, saying why: *"the renderer reaches no network and no device, every permission request is denied by the main process rather than granted (design section 5.1 rule 9)"*. `console/test/unit/entitlements.test.ts` parses the file and asserts the exact set, one named test per withheld key. It is unverified until the owner runs **Check 59a**.

**Consequence.** A helper that captures audio needs `com.apple.security.device.audio-input` on **that binary**, which means the entitlements story becomes two files with two different rules — and the test that pins "one key" must be extended rather than edited quietly.

**#17 — The universal build, the hardened runtime and the release workflow already exist; the nested binary does not. (Exists / Gap)**

`console/package.json`: `"package": "npm run build && electron-builder --mac --universal --publish never"`; `mac.hardenedRuntime: true`; `mac.entitlements` and `mac.entitlementsInherit` both `entitlements.mac.plist`; `appId io.github.dimipaun.fugaro-console`, `asar: true`. There is **no** `afterSign`, `notarize`, `extraResources` or `binaries` key — signing and notarisation live in `.github/workflows/console-release.yml`, and that file is **applied, not staged**: the owner copied the staged file in as plan Task 4.5 (`7fdfa61`, "Add console release workflow", 2026-10-17), and `main` carries the revision merged as `9b61d881` (PR #626), whose first tag is `v0.8.7` (`90ba554` — `v0.8.6` does not contain #626). The copy under `docs/pending-workflows/` is kept on purpose, not because it is still pending: `TestDocsConsoleReleaseWorkflowIsStagedAndPinned` reads it and asserts the applied file is byte-identical to it ("while both files exist, they are the same file"), which is also why the applied file's own header still opens with the staging note it was written with — that note is a leftover of the copy-in convention, and `docs/pending-workflows/README.md` states the status in words: *"staged by Task 4.2 … and applied by the owner as Task 4.5"*. **The revision matters here**: this document's baseline (`82ba600d`) predates #626, so the notarisation script below is named from the revision `main` carries now, as of 2026-10-19.

**Consequence.** A nested Swift helper must be **built for both arm64 and x86_64** (the app is universal), **signed with its own entitlements**, and **covered by the notarisation of the app** — and nothing in the pipeline does that for it. The workflow's own `codesign --sign` is on the `.dmg` alone; the bundle is signed by the packer, which the signing step hands `CSC_NAME` and `CSC_KEYCHAIN` with the hardened runtime on and `entitlements.mac.plist` as both `entitlements` and `entitlementsInherit`. Notarisation is **whole-bundle and once**: `console/scripts/notarize-and-staple.sh` (added by #626, merged as `9b61d881` — **after the baseline commit**, which has no `console/scripts/` directory at all; it is on `main` now, and it is the reason both packs run with `-c.mac.notarize=false`) zips the `.app` with `ditto -c -k --keepParent` and submits *that* a single time — so a nested binary is covered only by being signed and inside the bundle when the bundle is submitted; a helper that is unsigned, or that the packer leaves outside the bundle, is covered by no ticket this pipeline staples. Only the `xcrun stapler` calls are retried (five attempts, 15/30/60/90 s via `retry_apple_network`: the script's staple and validate, and the verification step's own validates, character for character the same helper); the submission is never retried, and neither is anything else. What the workflow checks about nested code is downstream of the submission and is what a helper has to survive: `codesign --verify --deep --strict` — `--deep` is what reaches the nested binary — plus `codesign -d --entitlements -`, `spctl --assess` and a `xcrun stapler validate` on the bundle **inside each shipped artifact** (the mounted `.dmg` and the extracted `.zip`), because the loose `.app` the packer left is in neither. That is a real, unpriced piece of work, and §5 Q6's answer — one helper, extended — is the one that has to buy it. And `docs/pending-workflows/` remains the only place a workflow change may be written from here (`.github/workflows/` may not be edited by an agent in this repository) — staged for the owner to copy in, as this one was.

**#18 — The console's write fence is unchanged by a talking interface, and it bites the moment the module acts. (Rule)**

`desktop-console.md` **D2** — *"Yes, strictly read-only"* — and v2 **D22** — *"One: launching, through `fugaro run`, behind the CLI's own budget pre-check and the app's own confirmation … Everything else stays read-only"* — with **D14**'s reasoning: *"Treat every identity as a possible admin, and give the read-only guarantee to the allow-list alone."* Voice's **D47**, as amended on 2026-10-17 with mobile **D128**, allows **cancel**, **approve/merge** and **confirming an action** by voice **only with a spoken read-back and an explicit "confirm"**, and refuses every other destructive verb (kill switch, budget writes, deletes) outright. Console launches require the confirmation (v2 D22 stands, stricter than the phone's D162).

**Consequence.** A module that only *talks* touches none of this, which is why §4 treats "the module speaks and never acts" as a resolution rather than a question. The moment a spoken command reaches `fugaro`, the module is a **third path to a confirmed action** and must go through `shared/confirm` — never around it (#43).

**#19 — The console's style sheet does not exist, and a voice screen's UI depends on it. (Gap)**

The `docs/design/console-styling.md` now on `main` (`721aa091`) states the situation in §1.1: *"There is **no `.css` file anywhere under `console/`**"*, `index.html` is script-only, `style-src 'self'` *"is already the directive a stylesheet needs, and it is not being used"*, and the design *"adds nothing to the CSP and removes nothing from it."* §1.3: the golden frames are *"pictures of unstyled markup … a picture of an absence."* The design is unapproved (Phase 6).

**Consequence.** The spec's "cloud icon on the voice screen" is an icon on a screen whose styling has no design and no implementation, in a window that refuses inline style (`style-src 'self'`, no `'unsafe-inline'`) and whose element factory deliberately has *"no style and no event handler"*. Any voice screen UI is downstream of #634.

### (c) The orchestrator, and where text goes in and out

**#20 — The spec's "orchestrator/LLM" box is not a Fugaro service, and no engine is chosen. (Wrong / Gap)**

The spec's pipeline names a middle box that the tree does not have as a service. `voice.md` **F12** states it: a **Fugaro run** is a cloud container per run and is *"not an orchestrator"*; the thing the earlier spec called "the Fugaro orchestrating agent (a Claude Code session)" is a **Claude Code session**, *"which is not a Fugaro construct at all"*; the tree's only `claude` invocation is `internal/agent/agent.go`'s `Args` — headless, one prompt per stage, in a container. The console's bridge to a session is **D40**, designed and unbuilt: the Agent SDK in a separate supervised helper process on the operator's own login.

Separately, **no STT or TTS engine is chosen anywhere**: mobile **D123** prescribes *"a measurement-first bake-off with a stated latency target"* whose candidates are *"the platform's own on-device engines (Apple's and Android's) plus at most one bundled engine"*, recorded by **Check 85**, which has not been run and whose text is not in the register.

**Consequence.** The spec's vendored resume (SpeechAnalyzer, whisper.cpp, Kokoro, Deepgram, AssemblyAI, Cartesia, ElevenLabs, Silero, openWakeWord, Porcupine) **pre-empts D123's bake-off**. §4 resolves that the spec's list is a *candidate list for the bake-off*, not a selection; §7 records which of the vendor claims are verifiable.

**#21 — VAD has no interface and no engine, and the spec's "audio frames in" has no carrier. (Gap)**

The ports merged with this change carry **no samples** — `AUDIO_IN_EVENT_FIELDS = ['kind']`, with `speech-start`, `speech-end` and `barge-in` — and its comment records why: D118's answer is that the app never stores or uploads audio. P3.4 names **no** VAD interface and no VAD engine; `docs/mobile/` does not contain the word "Silero" at all. `voice-spec.md` §5 has echo cancellation and VAD, and `voice.md`'s finding on them is that they are greenfield.

**Consequence.** "Silero VAD so only speech is transcribed" is new, and it is new in the one place the tree has an explicit architectural instruction: a VAD runs on **frames**, and frames exist only where capture happens — which D43 puts outside the renderer (#15). §5 Q2's decision chooses where frames live and therefore which VAD is possible at all.

**#22 — "Record raw session audio" is refused today, twice, in writing. (Rule)**

Mobile **D118**, answered: *"**The app never stores or uploads audio** — STT is on-device (D44), so the audio never leaves the device."* `voice.md` **D52**'s recommendation: audio fixtures are *"**synthesised, not captured**"*, *"because a recording of a person's voice is new stored material no redactor covers"* — and the exclusion that reasoning rests on is **F32**'s finding, which is why anything recorded needs a redaction check before it is committed. The design on `main` (`80f2b7fa`) applies the same rule in §4.5: *"the network is down | local only, immediately; **nothing is queued for later**, because a queued utterance is a recording, and D118's answer forbids one"*.

**Consequence.** The spec's *Engine toggle and experiments* bullet — "Record raw session audio to replay the same clips through each engine" — is not a small experiment; it is the one thing two documents forbid, and it is the only way to run the bake-off the spec wants. A bake-off needs **comparable inputs**: without recorded audio every engine is measured on different utterances. That tension is real; §5 Q8 asked the owner to resolve it, and the answer of 2026-10-19 is option (1) — no recording, synthesised clips, which is the rule **D52** already gives and the exclusion **F32** is behind.

**#23 — Where "text in / text out" attaches, in both directions. (Exists / Gap)**

- **Out (text to speak).** The feed is a **document**, not a subscription: `fugaro watch --json` emits one `JSONDoc` per change (`internal/watch/json.go`'s `BuildJSON`), and its consumer contract is written down on `main` in `console/docs/json-contract.md` §10 — thirteen facts, including §10.11 (*"Does option A need a supervisor process? Yes"*) and §10.13's reader rules, pinned by `internal/cli/docs_desktop_console_test.go`'s `TestDocsDesktopConsoleContractSettlesTheFeed` — the test `console/docs/json-contract.md` names for §10's facts, as it names `TestDocsDesktopConsoleContractNamesEveryWatchField` for §2's fields. The console's reader is `FugaroSource` (`desktop-console.md` §8.3). **The phone does not read this**: the reader on `main` (`ebe69147`) subscribes to **database nodes** (`mobile/src/feed/nodes.js`: *"The phone reads database **nodes**, not the console's `watch --json` document"*; `index.js` re-exports the directory's vocabulary and says in its own header that what a screen gets is *"not `nodes.js`'s raw readers or `paths.js`'s gate"*), over a closed family list (`FAMILIES = ['config/caps', 'config/kill', 'spend', 'mailbox']`, defined in `mobile/src/feed/paths.js` and re-exported by `index.js`).
- **In (text from the person).** For the phone this is the mailbox, and it exists on `main`: `shared/mailbox-schema` (with `validateMessage`, `macOf`/`verifyMac` and pinned `vectors.json`) and `shared/mailbox`. The `kind` field is `message` · `prompt` · `answer` · `action`; `text` is capped at 4096 code units and is **already redacted**; the command/replay window is `DEFAULT_WINDOW_MS = 6 * 60 * 60 * 1000` bounded by `MAX_WINDOW_MS = 7 days`; the prompt TTL is the owner's **O5**, `PROMPT_TTL_MS = 72 hours` on `main` (`f67753f9`). For the console it is **D40**'s helper, unbuilt, and the tree's only precedent for talking to Claude Code is the one-way, one-prompt-per-stage `internal/agent/agent.go` — which has **no `--input-format` flag at all**.

**Consequence.** "One common interface per direction" maps cleanly onto **an existing interface out (the feed, twice over, with two different sources) and an existing interface in (the mailbox for the phone; nothing for the console)**. The honest statement is that the *out* direction has one interface per consumer, not one interface overall, and the *in* direction has a working one only on the phone.

**#27 — Redaction exists, is ported, and is applied before the MAC — but it is best-effort. (Exists / Rule)**

The spec says nothing about this; §4 makes it a resolution.

### (e) The security service, and the temporary-token story

**#28 — The "per-project security service" is plan-only, and its scope is jobs, not clients. (Wrong / Exists)**

The spec says provider keys are stored *"at project level in the per-project security service"*. What exists is [docs/credentials-service/](../credentials-service/): README's status line *"**plan only; nothing is implemented**"*; a spec for a **standalone Cloud Run credential proxy** per project that is *"the only identity with Secret Manager access to the OAuth tokens and API keys"*; and an implementation plan in slices, of which the owner approved **C0 only, on 2026-10-18** (⊂ PR #584, whose Slice 0 is *"docs-only work, and one piece of rules groundwork that needs no rules change and no run-token grant"*).

Two of its decisions close the spec's reading. **Its scope is jobs**, and its own spec says so twice: its *Boundary* bullet reads *"the service stays secrets-only. Other always-on features (e.g. the phone client backend) go in sibling services that call it, keeping its attack surface minimal"*, and it says the service is *"the single holder of every secret a job needs"*. The design on `main` (`80f2b7fa`) quotes both at its Q2 and draws the conclusion the owner then took: a phone is not a job, so the hop is a separate service. And **D147** is opt-in per installation, with **D143** refusing a fallback: *"if the service is unreachable the run halts like `budget_unavailable` (M9 §12), **never falls back to an in-job key**."* Nothing in D130–D154 discusses storing or minting an STT/TTS provider credential.

**Consequence.** The spec's *Keys and security* section names a component for a job the component does not have: a **voice-provider key store**. Building one is a **new scope** for either the credentials service or a sibling, and the P5.3 design deliberately refused to assume it (#32).

**#29 — What exists today for provider keys, and what "project level" currently means. (Exists)**

- **Secret Manager, two scopes.** `internal/cli/secrets.go` implements `fugaro secrets` with `--project-wide` — the flag's own comment on `secretsOptions.projectWide`, in that file: *"stores or lists the secret once per Fugaro project rather than per repository (design docs/design/project-secrets.md §4)"* — with `gcp.SecretID(slug, logical)` and `gcp.ProjectSecretID(fugaroProject, logical)` (`internal/backend/gcp/names.go`) deriving every ID, and the reader rule from `docs/design/project-secrets.md`'s "Who reads" paragraph: *"Only a job's own service account, granted `secretAccessor` **per secret, per job**, exactly as today"*.
- **Three credential tiers.** [credentials-service/spec.md](../credentials-service/spec.md)'s *Credential tiers* section: **A** project-level named Claude OAuth tokens, **B** a direct Anthropic API key, **C** an OpenRouter key — the cascade its *Security service* section calls *"A, then B, then C"*. The *"only identity with Secret Manager access"* sentence is the **spec's** own (quoted at #28); **D148** is the decision that corrects it, ruling SECURITY.md say *"the only Fugaro runtime identity with access to model credentials"* and name the others, because *"the spec's wording overclaims"*. **D130** answers a different question — it supersedes *"Path B's mechanism and the held plan"* of the parked [mixed-credentials.md](../design/mixed-credentials.md) design, the subscription token in the reviewer stage, and keeps *"Paths A and C as tiers B and C"* — and **D131** is project-centric phase 5's "Mounting" and "Failover" being superseded.
- **Named OAuth tokens** ([named-oauth-tokens.md](../design/named-oauth-tokens.md), shipped in 0.6.5): `config.ReservedSecrets["claude-oauth-token"] = "CLAUDE_CODE_OAUTH_TOKEN"` — the name is not configurable — mounted per repository.
- **The in-runner gateway**: `internal/gateway`'s own package doc calls it *"a loopback proxy between Claude Code and the model API that holds the real credential"* (`Server.Token()`; `agent.GatewayVars` gives the agent *"never the real model credential"*, `internal/agent/env.go`).

**Consequence.** "Project level" is a **real and implemented** scope (`--project-wide`), so that half of the spec's sentence is right. The half that is wrong is *where* it lives: today it is Secret Manager reached by a job's own service account, not a "security service".

**#30 — The spec's per-session token minting does not exist, and the tree has three other minting mechanisms. (Gap / Exists)**

Nothing mints a Deepgram, AssemblyAI or Cartesia token. What the tree *does* mint, and these are the precedents a new minter would be modelled on:

- **The per-run budget token**: `internal/budget/token/mint.go`'s `Mint`/`MintAt` sign a Firebase custom token through IAM Credentials `signJwt` as a service account that holds no roles, with claims `fs`/`fr`/`fx`/`fp`/`rb`; `exchange.go`'s `Exchange` trades it for an ID token (*"minted more than an hour ago; launch the run again"*); `object.go`'s `PutObject` at `ObjectKey(slug, run)` and `token.TakeObject`, which *"deletes as it reads, so a token can be used once"*.
- **The gateway's per-run token**: `internal/gateway/gateway.go`'s `Server.Token()`.
- **GitHub App installation tokens**: `github.MintInstallationToken`, called from `internal/cli/gitcred.go` and `imagecheck.go`.

And one refusal worth reading first: `docs/design/m9-budget-and-dashboard.md` records that mint-on-start was considered and rejected, because making it safe *"needs **a minting endpoint that checks the caller's ID token and execution**, which is exactly the budget service D1 rejected."*

**Consequence.** The spec's minting idea is **architecturally familiar here and concretely absent**, and its safety hinges on the one thing the tree refused once already: a minting endpoint that authenticates a caller. The credentials-service work gives that caller a shape for **jobs** (**D134**: the run's existing budget identity; **D135**: reached through the in-runner gateway with a Cloud Run IAM invoker token for the service) — but **not for a laptop or a phone**. §5 Q12 asked which identity a voice client would mint as — the question D134/D135 deliberately did not answer — and the owner's answer of 2026-10-19 is that **nothing is minted**: the cloud half waits for that identity to be designed.

**#31 — The provider temporary tokens are real, and they are materially weaker per-session credentials than the spec implies. (Verified — see §6)**

All three providers support what the spec claims, and the details change the design:

- **Deepgram** — `POST /v1/auth/grant` returns a JWT with a **30-second default TTL** (`ttl_seconds` configurable), scope `usage::write` *"for core voice APIs"*, *"requiring an API key with Member or higher authorization"*, and *"Tokens created with this endpoint will not work with the Manage APIs."* Their own guide explains the pattern the spec wants: *"Direct access to the Deepgram API is particularly useful for client-side/untrusted applications that are more latency-sensitive"*, and instructs: *"Create a backend service to generate temporary tokens … Use tokens immediately after generation."*
- **AssemblyAI** — `GET /v3/token` takes **two** separate parameters whose distinction the spec's "per session" elides: `expires_in_seconds` (1–600) is the *"Token redemption window … the time the client has to use this token to open a WebSocket"*, and `max_session_duration_seconds` (60–10800, default 10800) *"does NOT cap the duration of the resulting streaming session"* — that is what does. And it is **not single-use**: *"until this window elapses, the same token can open multiple sessions, including concurrent sessions."*
- **Cartesia** — `POST /access-token` with granular **grants** (`tts`, `stt`, `agent`), each mapping to named endpoints, and `expires_in` **capped at one hour** (*"The maximum is 1 hour (3600 seconds)"*), generated server-side from an API key, with the client passing it as a query parameter on the browser WebSocket.

**Consequence.** Four things the spec's *Keys and security* section should say and does not: a token's **TTL is short (30 s to 1 h), not session-length**, so a long conversation re-mints and the client must handle the exchange mid-session; AssemblyAI's token is **reusable within its window**, so "one session" is a property of the *session duration parameter*, not of the token; the **scope is per-capability**, so the minter must grant `tts` and not `stt` to a client that only speaks; and **minting requires a powerful credential** (Deepgram demands Member or higher), which is precisely the long-lived key the spec says must never be on a device — so the minter is a service that *holds* it. §5 Q12, answered by the owner on 2026-10-19: option (1), nothing minted.

**#32 — The interim the brief asks about is not free, and the tree has already refused its closest shape once. (Rule)**

The brief asks "what happens with **no** security service yet: an interim: a Secret Manager secret and a small token-minting function". The tree's record:

- **D130**'s alternative column names an interim and the owner **did not take it**: *"**Keep B as the interim** (token in the reviewer stage of the job) until the service exists"*.
- **D131**'s answer keeps a refusal in place: *"Task 3.4a's refusal stays until the service exists."*
- `docs/design/project-named-oauth-tokens.md` (⊂ PR #584, and on neither that branch's base nor `main`) calls the current carrier exactly that: *"**The carrier is a stopgap** … *because it is temporary*: it will be replaced by the planned **security service**."*
- `docs/design/m9-budget-and-dashboard.md` §592 records that a naive minting endpoint was **considered and rejected**.

**Consequence.** A small minting function is not a neutral placeholder: it holds a **Member-or-higher provider key** in a new service, is **always-on** (a Deepgram token lasts 30 seconds, so the function must be reachable at every connect), and needs the caller authentication D134/D135 defined for jobs and not for clients. §5 Q12 offered the thinner interim — **no cloud voice at all until the decision is taken** — as the recommended option, because it is the option that adds no credential; that is the option the owner took on 2026-10-19.

### (g) Barge-in, echo cancellation, and where the audio graph lives

**#37 — Barge-in is already specified with a number, and echo cancellation is already known to be greenfield. (Exists)**

`voice.md`'s acceptance criterion 5: *"Speaking over the assistant stops playback within ~300 ms."* `voice-spec.md` §5: *"Echo cancellation so the assistant's own speech isn't transcribed"* and *"Barge-in: user speech during TTS stops playback immediately and starts a new turn."* The plan's **V5.2** is the task that must either implement it or be honest: *"echo cancellation, or an honest statement that there is none … if there is none, **suppresses capture while playing and says so**."* The `AudioIn` merged with this change already carries a `barge-in` event kind. **Check 86** reserves the owner-run measurement.

**Consequence.** The spec's *Barge-in* section is a **restatement plus one new requirement** — the spec's *Cleanup* line, *"record how much of the reply was actually heard and pass that to the agent"* — which is new: nothing in the tree records an interrupted utterance's extent beyond "interrupted". The `TextToSpeech.speak` merged with this change resolves either `{ ok: true, spoken: true }` or `interrupted`, with no measure. §4 makes "the extent is recorded" a resolution.

**#38 — The spec's echo-cancellation design is right, and it collides with both D43 and the CSP. (Rule)**

The spec's rule — *"TTS must play through the same audio pipeline the canceller monitors (not a separate native player)"* — is correct echo-cancellation engineering, and in this tree it is a **cross-process** problem. `getUserMedia({ audio: { echoCancellation: true } })` is a renderer API; **D43 puts capture outside the renderer** (#15); and the renderer's CSP has `connect-src 'none'` and no `media-src` (#14). So "the same pipeline" cannot be the renderer's Web Audio graph unless the renderer gains both capture and network — which is the one-window amendment the owner refused.

**Consequence.** The spec's architecture has a **split-brain**: the canceller must see both the microphone and the loudspeaker, and this tree's decisions put the microphone in a helper and (for Kokoro, #39) the synthesis wherever it lands. The workable shapes are all consequences of §5 Q2: **(i)** both capture and playback in one helper process, with the renderer out of the audio path entirely — the only shape that keeps `connect-src 'none'`; **(ii)** an OS-level AEC the helper can use, which is exactly the spec's own Apple/Android sentence, leaving the renderer out; or **(iii)** audio in the renderer with both loosened, refused by D43.

**#39 — Kokoro in the renderer needs `'wasm-unsafe-eval'`, which the CSP does not have. (Rule)**

A WASM/ONNX Kokoro runs in the renderer only if the CSP allows WebAssembly compilation: `script-src 'self'` does not, and `'wasm-unsafe-eval'` is absent — as the console's own code notes twice (`bundle.test.ts`: *"the CSP has no `'unsafe-eval'`"*; `tools/bundle-renderer/index.ts` the same). Adding it is a **second, independent loosening** from #14's, bought for a different reason.

### (h) Logging, metrics, and the phone's remaining items

### (i) The confirmation boundary

**#43 — The confirmation machine exists on `main`, and this module must never be a second path to it. (Exists / Rule)**

`shared/confirm` is merged. Its rules, verbatim from its README: *"A confirmation must be a **separate utterance** recognised as the **fixed phrase**; it has a **timeout**; **one confirmation authorises exactly one action**, which is … **the utterance that issued a command can never confirm it**."* Its constants: `DESTRUCTIVE_VERBS = ['cancel', 'confirm', 'merge']`, `PASS_THROUGH_VERBS = ['launch']` (D162: a launch is **not** confirmed on the phone), `REFUSED_VERBS` — a closed list including `budget`, `budget-kill`, `kill-switch`, `delete`, `revoke`, `purge` — `CONFIRM_PHRASES = CONFIRMATION_PHRASES` (imported from P6.1, *"so the router and this module cannot disagree"*), `DEFAULT_CONFIRM_TIMEOUT_MS = 60 * 1000` (bounds 1 s to 5 min), and `CONFIRM_CUE = 'Say "Fugaro, confirm" to confirm.'` The phrase set is deliberately **not** `yes`: *"'yes', 'sure', 'ok', 'yeah', 'confirmed' and 'confirmed it' are deliberately not in it."*

**Consequence.** This is the answer to the brief's item (k), and it is a **Rule with no options**: a voice interface that summarises events **speaks**; it never recognises a confirmation. A spoken "yes" confirms nothing here, and a module that accepted one would be a second path around `shared/confirm`'s four properties (separate utterance, fixed phrase, timeout, one-action-per-confirmation). This module's own barge-in and interrupt paths must also never *appear* to confirm — see **R13**.

**#44 — Check numbering: what is written, what is held, and what a new check would take. (Exists / Rule)**

The register `docs/gcp-live-checklist.md` states the discipline: *"a reserved number is never reused or renumbered."* For this work: **64** (console voice, owner-run, written by V8.2 — **not run**), **65** (signed-build microphone check, held), **66** (the cloud-provider check, *"that can exist only if the owner takes the second egress decision"*, held), **85** (the on-device STT/TTS bake-off — *"not run, and its text is not in the register"*, written by P4.2), **86** (background audio and barge-in), **102** (wake-word recognition rate, proposed, Phase 6 not approved), **103** (a spoken cancel and merge on the sandbox repository). Checks 90–99 belong to the credentials service Slice 0 (⊂ PR #584: 90 is written and was run on 2026-10-21 — PASSED, TIER A VIABLE — and 91 is neither written nor run); the design on `main` (`80f2b7fa`) names **110** as the next free number.

**Consequence.** A **console** voice bake-off has no number, and D44's "test both" cannot be recorded against 66 without the egress decision (66 is held *for* that decision) or 85 (85 is on-device, phone). This document **allocates nothing** — §5 Q13 asked whether the console's multi-engine comparison joins 66, joins 85, or takes a new number, because the owner owns the register; the owner answered on 2026-10-19 with option (1), a table in this folder's design and no register entry yet.

## 4. Resolutions

Findings with one defensible answer. They need no decision from the owner; they need to be written into the design, and each already has a citation that makes it true.

**R1 — The module's interfaces are the ports that exist, not new ones.** Build on `shared/command` (routing), `shared/confirm` (confirmations), `shared/digest` (severity + redaction) and, for text in, `shared/mailbox`/`shared/mailbox-schema`; the three ports merged with this change are the audio-side surface. Where a port is missing (`AudioOut`, a VAD, a metrics sink), add it **to that set** and not beside it. (#2, #21, #23, #41)

**R2 — The classifier is `shared/digest`'s ladder, extended by one rung.** `SEVERITY` (`kill`, `failed`, `fast_burn`, `info`) already exists and already carries two urgent reasons the spec does not name (`fast_burn`, `offline`). The spec's "mention" rung is the single addition; the spec's "silent" tier is the ladder's default and needs no code. (#9, #12)

**R3 — The interrupt switch is `shared/prompts`' `SWITCH`, not a new toggle.** Three positions, local-only, never written to the database, with `INTERRUPT.PROMPT` covering the spec's "agent asking a question" the moment a producer exists. (#12, #25)

**R4 — Redaction happens at the boundary, before the model and before the speaker.** Apply `RedactFunc`/`redactFunc` to every string that leaves — the summariser's input, the spoken line, any log line — and never to the audio or only to the transcript. Reuse the ported `shared/digest/redact.js`; do not write a second redactor. (#27)

**R5 — Metrics are local, aggregate-only and textless.** Counts, durations, percentiles and battery readings may be logged; a partial transcript, a summary, a title or a spawn argv beyond D20's four fields may not. Owner-run, on the operator's own machine, like Check 64's step 3. (#41)

**R6 — The module speaks and never acts.** Nothing in the spec's sections proposes an action, and the tree's fence (D2, v2 D22, D14) grants exactly one write to the console under the CLI's own pre-check. A talking module touches none of it; an acting one goes through `shared/confirm` and nothing else. This is the module's answer to item (k). (#18, #43)

**R7 — The wake lock is a task, not a decision.** "Keep the screen awake on the voice screen" has no home in the mobile work; it belongs to P3.5's scope and needs no ruling. (#42)

**R8 — `docs/voice-module/` cites the seven points and does not restate them.** Conditions are read at `docs/mobile/design/voice-evaluation-switch.md`; this folder owns what the module does. (§1)

**R9 — The spec's vendor list is a candidate list for the bake-off, not a selection.** D123/D46 prescribe a measurement-first bake-off with a stated latency target; naming engines before Check 85 reports is the thing D123 exists to prevent. (#20)

**R10 — The vetted replacement for `speak(text)`'s "interrupted" is an extent.** The `TextToSpeech.speak` merged with this change resolves `{ ok: true, spoken: true }` or `interrupted`; the spec's "record how much of the reply was actually heard and pass that to the agent" needs a third field on that resolve (or its mirror in the console), and the interruption path must set it. (#37)

**R11 — No binary blob enters the tree without a licence policy.** The tree tracks no `.onnx`/`.gguf`/`.tflite`/`.wasm` and has no licence gate (#36). Whichever engines survive §5 Q5, the commit that adds the first weights adds the policy with them. (#36)

**R12 — Four of the spec's claims are corrected by the providers' own words**, and the corrections are in §6: Deepgram's token is 30 seconds by default; AssemblyAI's is a redemption window plus a separate session cap and is **not** single-use; Cartesia's is capped at one hour; and all three require a **powerful credential** to mint. The spec's *Keys and security* section should carry these four corrections. (#31)

**R13 — The wake word never confirms, and the module speaks no cue that invites a "yes".** Two closed sets must stay disjoint and neither may be widened: `shared/command`'s `WAKE_ALIASES` (`['fugaro', 'figaro']`) wakes the app, and `CONFIRMATION_PHRASES` — re-exported by `shared/confirm` as `CONFIRM_PHRASES` *"so the router and this module cannot disagree"* — confirms an action. `shared/confirm`'s four properties are the rule: a confirmation is a **separate utterance** carrying the **fixed phrase**, it is **time-bounded**, it authorises **exactly one** action, and *"the utterance that issued a command can never confirm it"*. So a spoken "Fugaro, cancel that" may never double as its own confirmation, an acoustic wake word may never be read as one (#7), and this module's barge-in and interrupt paths must not *appear* to confirm. The only sentence the module may speak about confirming is the cue itself — `CONFIRM_CUE = 'Say "Fugaro, confirm" to confirm.'` — and §5 rule 3 of the design on `main` (`80f2b7fa`) keeps the read-back and the confirmation **entirely local**, so even under the switch the confirming utterance is never transcribed remotely. (#7, #43)
